About Me

I got into data through a fairly ordinary frustration: I could apply a technique correctly and still not be able to say why it worked, and that bothered me more than it should have. That turned into a habit of writing detailed notes on everything I study - not summaries, but full derivations. It's why I can tell you not just that Lasso zeroes out coefficients while Ridge doesn't, but why - one has λ in the numerator, the other in the denominator.

I'd rather spend an extra hour understanding why a method works than memorise when to use it - slower up front, but it's the only way I've found to actually debug a model instead of guessing at hyperparameters until something improves.

That habit shows up directly in how I build projects - I tend to document the debugging story, not just the final metric. On my kidney CT classification project, that meant noticing a model was unstable at a learning rate of 0.01, systematically dropping it to 0.001, and tracking each experiment through DVC and MLflow until evaluation accuracy went from 69% to 91%. That process taught me more than any tutorial could.

I came into ML from mechanical engineering, including six months running a maintenance team at an industrial plant - not a typical background for this field, but one that left me comfortable making decisions from incomplete data under real operational pressure. I'm now looking for a team where I can keep building toward production ML systems: the kind with a Docker container and a reproducible pipeline behind them, not just a notebook.
