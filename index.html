About Me

My interest in data began with a curiosity about how meaningful insights can be extracted from information and transformed into better decisions. Over time, that curiosity evolved into a focused pursuit of statistics, machine learning, and analytical problem-solving.

Today, I continue to strengthen my foundation across probability theory, statistical inference, data analysis, and machine learning. I believe that understanding the reasoning behind methods is just as important as knowing how to apply them.

I strongly believe that true understanding comes from being able to explain concepts clearly and derive them, not just apply them. Before using a library function, I like to work through the math behind it — why squared error corresponds to Gaussian noise under MLE, why L1 regularisation creates sparsity while L2 doesn't, why Random Forest's node-level feature sampling decorrelates trees more than plain bagging. I keep detailed written notes on everything I study, and I regularly revisit and rewrite them as my understanding deepens.

Coming from a mechanical engineering and industrial operations background, I bring a habit of structured problem-solving and comfort working with real operational data — a perspective I think is genuinely useful in applied ML, not just a detour from it. I'm currently looking for opportunities where I can apply this foundation to real data-driven problems, work with experienced engineers, and keep building toward production-grade ML systems.
